> **Gemini sometimes appears to over-infer stance from contextual association.**

The biggest examples are **22, 27, 35, 52, 57, 67, 93, and 99**.

In these cases, the tweet mentions or discusses the three-term system but does not necessarily express a position toward it.

That distinction is **exactly what matters for stance detection**.

# THE MOST IMPORTANT PATTERN IN THIS BATCH

This batch reveals something **very important about the three models**.

Gemini has a recurring tendency to interpret:

> **negative context around the target**

as:

> **negative stance toward the target.**

That happens in:

* **226**
* **228**
* **232**
* **239**
* **242**
* **246**
* **249**

But several of those are actually **None or Favor**.

For example:

> “نبغى الفصل الثالث كامل عن بعد”

The word/context may involve dissatisfaction with the current arrangement, but the author's position toward **having a third term** is actually supportive.

GPT does well when the stance is:

* explicit;
* sarcastic;
* clearly positive/negative;
* tied directly to the target.

# 5. Where Qwen is stronger

This is where Qwen is particularly impressive.

Qwen is more conservative with:

* factual statements;
* questions;
* market information;
* technical descriptions;
* target mentions without explicit stance.

That is exactly why Qwen gets **90/100** here.

Its main weakness is that it can sometimes become **too conservative about implicit Favor**, which is visible in examples such as **331, 354, 358, and potentially 394**.

# 6. Where Gemini is stronger

Gemini remains strong on:

* explicit stance;
* emotionally charged tweets;
* sarcasm;
* strong praise;
* strong criticism.

But the repeated weakness is now becoming quite clear:

### **Target mention ≠ target stance**

Gemini sometimes interprets:

> “Here is information about electric vehicles”

as:

> “I support electric vehicles.”

And sometimes:

> “Here is a criticism of one property of electric vehicles”

as:

> “I oppose electric vehicles.”

That is dangerous for Track 2.
